Craving Intervention Manual

Rewiring the Non-Conscious Brain

Willpower is a limited resource. To truly alleviate cravings, we must stop fighting them and instead update the brain's internal Reward Value. This manual combines reinforcement learning with mindfulness to structurally dismantle habits.

Phase 1: Awareness

Make the unconscious conscious. Identify cues and map the habit loop.

Phase 2: Reward Value

Update the brain's data. Prove that the old habit is actually unrewarding.

Phase 3: Rewiring

Install a new "Bigger, Better Offer" driven by curiosity and mastery.

Phase 2: Reward Value Update

The Hacking

The goal here is to teach the non-conscious brain (specifically the orbitofrontal cortex) that the old habit is unrewarding. We do this through Negative Prediction Error—noticing that the expected reward doesn't match the actual experience.

Step 2.1: Urge Surfing

Cravings are not static. Like a wave, they rise, peak, and fall. If you fight (Red), the struggle persists. If you observe (Blue), it subsides naturally.

Step 2.2: The Disenchantment Practice

If you indulge, do it mindfully. Notice the gap between what you expected and what you got.

Simulation: The "Bite" Effect

"The dopamine signal that used to say 'Do this again!' now says 'Wait, that wasn't worth it.'"

Phase 3: The Rewiring

Conditioning the "Bigger, Better Offer"

The brain needs a new, higher-value reward to replace the old habit. We replace the routine with Curiosity and the reward with Mastery & Freedom. This is positive conditioning.

Step 3.1: The 5-Minute Block

When the Cue hits, substitute the habit with 5 minutes of curious observation. "I am allowing the sensation to be here."
